Vexomiser

Summary

We replaced the IC-based similarity in Exomiser's HiPhive, Phive, and PhenIX with INDIGENA's embedding-based BMA, and evaluated three training configs (MGI-only, HP-only, MGI+HP) on PAVS Track 1 (518 test cases) and Track 2 (284 test cases, phenotype + variant). On Track 1, INDIGENA variants perform on par with their baselines; HP-only is the strongest config. On Track 2, all configs improve MR, Hits@10, Hits@100, and AUC; INDIGENA-Phive (MGI-only) achieves the best MR (9.9), Hits@10 (0.937), Hits@100 (0.972), and AUC (0.959).

Background

We trained INDIGENA on the PAVS dataset and evaluated its phenotype similarity against Exomiser's existing methods. The integration replaces only the similarity function in each prioritiser; gene-phenotype model associations (OMIM, MGI, ZFIN) remain unchanged.

Methods

Benchmark and Evaluation Setup

We used the PAVS benchmark, Track 1 (5,076 cases, phenotype-only gene ranking). Cases were split train/val/test (80/10/10) using a disease-disjoint strategy (all cases sharing the same MONDO disease ID go to the same partition), stratified by cohort (Saudi, DDD, Mixed). The gene pool was fixed to all 2,258 unique genes across the full dataset. All results are reported on the test split (518 cases).

Graph Construction

INDIGENA was trained on a knowledge graph built from four cumulative components:

LayerContent
G1UPheno OWL2Vec* projection: 771K triples covering HP, MP, and UPHENO bridging axioms
G2Gene → phenotype associations (see below)
G3Disease → HP associations from training cases only; test/val diseases excluded to preserve inductiveness
G4Gene ↔ disease associations from training cases (supervised GDA signal)

Gene phenotype source (G2). We trained three configurations differing only in G2: (1) MGI-only: MGI mouse-knockout MP phenotypes mapped to human genes via HomoloGene; genes with no mouse ortholog receive no G2 entry; (2) HP-only: training-case HPO annotations per gene; only genes appearing in training cases receive a G2 entry; (3) MGI+HP: MGI MP primary with training-case HPO additive. In all three configs, OMIM disease–gene associations are excluded from G2: they encode the same GDA signal supervised in G4, and including them would leak the training target.

Training

INDIGENA was trained using TransD KGE (100-dimensional embeddings, 300 epochs, Adam optimiser, batch size 2,048, learning rate 0.001) on a GPU. Three configurations were trained, varying only the G2 gene→phenotype source (see Standalone INDIGENA results below).

Integration into Exomiser

To use INDIGENA as a drop-in similarity function within Exomiser, we introduced a ModelScorerFactory interface that abstracts the phenotype similarity computation away from the prioritiser classes. Both HiPhivePriority and PhivePriority were updated to accept a factory at construction time, defaulting to the original Phenodigm behaviour.

IndigenaModelScorerFactory implements this interface: given a patient's HP terms and a gene model's phenotype terms (HP or MP), it computes the INDIGENA BMA score from pre-exported embedding vectors.

PhenixPriority does not expose a pluggable similarity factory. As an approximation, INDIGENA-PhenIX uses HiPhivePriority with runParams("human") (human disease models only, no cross-species lookup, no PPI) with the INDIGENA BMA scorer substituted.

Evaluation modes: per-disease vs. merged

Merged (mg) treats each gene as a single bag of phenotype terms: score(gene) = BMA(patient_HPs, gene_phenos).

Per-disease (pd) scores each gene–disease model separately and takes the maximum: score(gene) = max_d BMA(patient_HPs, disease_d_HPs). This mirrors Exomiser's behaviour. The distinction applies only to the HP component; MGI phenotypes are always merged.

The five eval modes are:

HP-only training produces no MGI embeddings, so eval_mgi, eval_mgi_hp_mg, and eval_mgi_hp_pd are not applicable for that config.

Discussion

Integration scope. INDIGENA replaces only the pairwise phenotype similarity function inside BMA; gene-disease model associations remain fixed by Exomiser's database.

Training configuration (Track 1). Standalone INDIGENA outperforms the Exomiser integration on Track 1 (best MRR 0.281 vs 0.272). Within the integration, HP-only gives the best results (e.g. INDIGENA-PhenIX MRR 0.272 vs PhenIX baseline 0.259). MGI+HP is consistently the worst: INDIGENA-HiPhive drops to MRR 0.261 and MR 610, and INDIGENA-Phive falls to MRR 0.005. INDIGENA-Phive underperforms across all configs because Phive requires HP↔MP cross-species comparison, where Phenodigm's explicitly calibrated IC mapping outperforms INDIGENA's implicit UPheno alignment.

Track 2. Adding variant scores improves all methods substantially (HiPhive MRR ~0.27 → 0.58). INDIGENA methods improve MR and recall (Hits@10, Hits@100, AUC) but lower MRR and Hits@1 relative to their baselines. For example, INDIGENA-HiPhive (MGI-only) reduces MR from 18.2 to 10.5, meaning the causal gene is ranked higher on average, while MRR drops from 0.580 to 0.466, meaning fewer cases land at rank 1. INDIGENA-Phive (MGI-only) shows the sharpest improvement in MR (28.0 to 9.9) and achieves the best Hits@10 (0.937), Hits@100 (0.972), and AUC (0.959) of any method.

Limitations. Exomiser gates scoring on known gene-disease associations; genes absent from the database score zero regardless of phenotypic similarity, leaving INDIGENA's inductive capability unexploited. The Track 2 combined score is a simple product; a learned combination may recover top-1 precision without sacrificing recall.

Next Steps

  • Score combination: investigate learned or calibrated combination of variant and phenotype scores to recover top-1 precision without sacrificing Hits@10 recall
  • Multihop-GDA integration: add the Multihop-GDA method as a second ModelScorerFactory implementation and compare against INDIGENA on both tracks
